# Guided Query Questionnaire

Use this as a routing guide, not a form to dump on the author. First read the complete novel, extract factual answers, and derive a positioning profile. Then ask only for confirmation, correction, missing facts, or consequential choices in short conversational rounds.

## Prerequisite refresh gate

Before starting the rounds, inspect the current conversation and allowed query-package state for established prerequisite information. Show the author a compact list of what is available, when it was approved or sourced, any changed manuscript indicator, and any open items. Ask whether to reuse it, refresh all of it, or refresh named fields only. Pause for the answer unless the current request already states the choice.

Do not make the author repeat established answers after they choose reuse. Do not let reuse preserve time-sensitive agent research; always reverify current agent and agency submission facts for a named-agent package.

## Interview sequence

### Round 1: Goal and source

Establish:

- which novel and manuscript version the package represents;
- whether the user wants a master package, a named-agent submission, or both;
- whether the user already has a specific agent in mind and, if so, the agent's profile, agency, or submission-guidelines link;
- target market or territory, if already decided;
- any deadline or submission window.

If the manuscript and existing materials are available, read them before continuing.

### Round 2: Book identity and provisional classification

Confirm:

- title and optional subtitle;
- the manuscript-derived adult, young adult, middle grade, or other category;
- the manuscript-derived primary genre and useful secondary positioning;
- exact or rounded word count;
- completion and revision status;
- original language and query language;
- whether any version has been posted, self-published, or traditionally published;
- prior agent or publisher submission history when relevant.

Explain that publication and submission history can affect available rights and disclosure obligations.

### Round 3: Story engine

Acquire enough information to answer:

- Who is the principal protagonist?
- What destabilizes the protagonist's existing life?
- What concrete outcome do they pursue?
- What person, institution, force, or inner conflict opposes them?
- What difficult choice or escalation makes the story distinctive?
- What is lost if they fail?
- What causal turn drives the final act?
- How does the novel end?

Derive these from the manuscript when possible. Ask the author to resolve competing protagonists, concealed premises, or intentionally ambiguous endings when they materially affect the pitch.

Before presenting the answers, reconcile the manuscript's chronology, entities, object provenance, causal links, motives, knowledge states, revelation timing, and ending. Classify each consequential proposition as `fact`, `character-belief`, `inference`, or `ambiguous`. Do not convert adjacency into causation or a character's assertion into objective truth.

### Round 4: Manuscript-derived reader promise and positioning

Present an evidence-based proposal for the author to confirm or correct:

- target and crossover readership;
- prose essence: register, cadence, density, narrative distance, interiority, and tonal character;
- dominant story type or narrative engine;
- tone;
- central themes or questions;
- two or three comparable books, authors, films, or other works;
- what each comparison demonstrates: audience, voice, structure, premise, or tone;
- the novel's differentiating element.

For each consequential field, state the manuscript evidence, confidence, credible alternative classifications, and how the finding should change the query's hook, plot emphasis, diction, worldbuilding, metadata, or comparisons. Do not make the author define these fields from scratch.

Do not infer readership from protagonist age alone, genre from setting or isolated tropes, story type from a rigid universal taxonomy, or prose essence from a single passage. Do not require the author to supply comps unaided. Offer researched candidates when requested. Verify current facts before recommending them, and explain both the fit and limitation of each candidate.

### Round 5: Agent discovery and priority review

Use the confirmed or provisional positioning profile to research a broad candidate pool. If author information was supplied or acquired alongside the manuscript, include relevant author-approved facts in matching and priority; otherwise use manuscript positioning and verified agent evidence alone. Verify viable candidates through current official agent or agency sources, and present:

- an ordered active shortlist grouped into `Priority 1`, `Priority 2`, and `Priority 3`;
- the specific manuscript-positioning evidence matched by each agent's documented interests or list;
- author-agent matching evidence only when the supporting author information was available at intake and approved for this use;
- current open status, represented categories, exclusions, territory, and agency concurrency constraints;
- official source URLs, supplementary evidence when useful, and access dates;
- a concise fit rationale, limitation, and reason for the assigned priority;
- a separate watchlist for closed, uncertain, or otherwise ineligible candidates.

Ask the author to confirm exclusions, disclose prior submissions to shortlisted agents or agencies, and select which candidates should receive tailored materials. State whether author information was used in the ranking. Make clear that the ordering is a research recommendation, not a prediction of representation.

### Round 6: Author information

Ask only for relevant information:

- preferred professional name and pronouns if needed;
- location at the level the author is comfortable disclosing;
- publication credits, awards, education, expertise, or professional experience;
- lived, cultural, linguistic, or research connection the author wants to mention;
- website or platform only when relevant;
- previous traditionally published books;
- debut status.

Never pressure the author to disclose protected or private identity. A short biography with no publishing credentials is valid for debut fiction. Information first acquired in this round supports biography and tailoring, but does not retroactively become ranking evidence unless the author explicitly requests a refreshed shortlist.

### Round 7: Series architecture

When the book is connected to other volumes, establish:

- planned number of books;
- whether Book One resolves its principal dramatic movement;
- which questions or conflicts remain open;
- whether later books are drafted;
- whether the author would revise the series structure for publication;
- the one-paragraph trajectory of each later volume.

Classify honestly as one of:

- standalone;
- standalone with series potential;
- first in a planned series;
- first in a required multi-volume story.

Do not use `standalone with series potential` merely because it sounds safer.

### Round 8: Named-agent tailoring

For each named agent, establish or research:

- the agent, agency, or submission-guidelines URL supplied by the author, when available;
- official name, agency, and submission channel;
- direct web-form URL or verified submission email address;
- open or closed status;
- represented categories and exclusions;
- requested query fields, synopsis length, and sample pages;
- exact website field labels, required status, limits, and the package content mapped to each field;
- for email, the required recipient, subject line, body order, attachments, filenames, and formats;
- attachment, upload, or paste rules and accepted file types;
- simultaneous-submission and one-agent-per-agency rules;
- response policy;
- post-submission tracking details;
- a genuine personalization basis.

Ask the author to paste the link or official agent or agency email when one is available. Treat it as the starting point for customization, then confirm that it is current and official. If no link is available, locate the official agent or agency page and direct submission route. Research the steps instead of asking the author to reconstruct them. Ask only for author-held information that cannot be obtained from official sources.

Convert the verified requirements into a numbered submission sequence and a field-or-email mapping before packaging. If official instructions conflict or the direct route cannot be confirmed, identify the exact conflict, mark the named-agent package `not-ready`, and do not guess.

## Confirmation record

Before drafting, show:

```markdown
## Confirmed
- ...

## Proposed positioning
- Category and genre:
- Target readership:
- Prose essence:
- Story type:
- Tone and reader promise:
- Query implications:

## Inferences requiring confirmation
- ...

## Manuscript fact ledger
| Proposed wording or atomic claim | Type | Manuscript location | Confidence or alternative | Confirmation or required qualification |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ... | fact / character-belief / inference / ambiguous | ... | ... | ... |

## Open but non-blocking
- ...

## Planned artifacts
- ...

## Proposed agent priorities
- Author information used in ranking: <intake source and approved factors | none available>
- Priority 1:
- Priority 2:
- Priority 3:
- Watchlist or exclusions:
- Selected tailoring targets:
```

Require separate confirmation for facts that affect rights, debut status, category, series dependency, story outcome, biography, or agent personalization, and for every consequential inference the package would present as settled. Approval of the positioning summary or package direction is not blanket approval of unlisted plot claims. Allow drafting with clearly marked open items for optional comps, platform links, or agent selection, but do not draft story-side package prose until the manuscript fact ledger is approved. Once the ledger is approved, it supports both the general package and any requirements-driven agent-specific synopsis; do not add a separate synopsis-approval round.
