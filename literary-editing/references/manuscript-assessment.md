# Manuscript Assessment

Use this workflow when the author wants an editorial diagnosis, readiness assessment, or recommendation about the next kind of editing.

## Scope and readiness

A final assessment requires the complete manuscript in reading order. If only a sample, synopsis, or selected chapters are available, label the result `provisional` and state which whole-book questions remain unanswered.

Confirm the reviewed Git commit SHA and manuscript paths before reading. Do not edit the manuscript and do not create sentence-level change proposals during assessment.

## Review method

1. Read the complete manuscript in order before finalizing the verdict.
2. Establish the manuscript's apparent aims, audience, genre or category, and current stage.
3. Evaluate:
   - premise and narrative promise;
   - structure, pacing, and escalation;
   - character development and motivation;
   - point of view and information control;
   - thematic coherence and emotional effect;
   - prose strengths and recurring weaknesses;
   - continuity or plausibility risks;
   - genre and reader expectations;
   - readiness for developmental editing, line editing, copyediting, or production.
4. Support each significant finding with locations and representative evidence.
5. Rank findings by impact rather than manuscript order.

## Verdict

Return a verdict using `editorial-state.md` with:

- scope and readiness;
- a concise overall diagnosis;
- the manuscript's strongest working elements;
- three to seven prioritized findings;
- evidence locations for each finding;
- risks or unknowns;
- a recommended next service and why;
- any author decisions needed before proceeding.

Use `pass`, `pass-with-notes`, `revise`, or `not-ready` only as editorial readiness judgments, not as judgments of the author's worth or the book's market success.

## Boundaries

An assessment identifies what kind of work is needed; it is not an exhaustive developmental edit. Do not prescribe a rigid story template, generate a line-edit diff, silently rewrite passages, or claim whole-book completion from partial material. Assessment normally produces no manuscript diff.
