Reality Benders.  
As the name suggests, they possess the power to distort reality. To them, established facts are like clay, easily molded at will.  
—But precisely because of this, they absolutely cannot change the “future” that has not yet become fact.

The girl’s power was very strong, so strong that her wish attracted the favor of the God of Fate.  
Therefore, the girl’s wish would be fulfilled personally by Fate.

“Ah… help… me…”  
The girl was still pleading hoarsely for help.  
She had already heard the continuous gunfire and soldiers’ shouts outside the containment chamber. As those sounds gradually faded, she foresaw a terrifying truth: someone had killed all the guards just to take her away.

“But what if those guards were actually…”  
The girl thought of a more optimistic possibility: just like in those fairy tales, she was a princess imprisoned, and the prince coming to rescue her was fighting through countless obstacles.  
That was the only way she could think of it. The girl hid under the covers, covering her head, hoping not to be taken away—  
And just as she mustered the courage to peek out from under the covers to see what was happening, the wall of the containment chamber was violently blown apart.

“May I ask, are you the one who called me?”  

The girl sat on the hospital bed, staring blankly at the “prince” who had broken through the wall opposite her…  
Oh, not a prince.  
The other party was a white-haired young woman.

“Um… nice to meet you?” the woman greeted, “Just call me Scotty.”  
Then she walked over to the girl’s bedside.  
“To cut a long story short, I’m going to carry you out of here—just the two of us.”

Still stunned and unable to respond, the woman who called herself Scotty reached out her hand:  
“What are you waiting for, our dear little princess?”  
“Eh?”  
Before the girl could say more, Scotty scooped her up and dashed out of the Site-19 containment chamber where she had lived since childhood.

As Scotty carried her away, the girl felt a different kind of warmth. After all, even before she lost consciousness, no one had ever voluntarily hugged her, as if they feared her abilities…  
But this person was different—her intuition told her so.

Then, she looked back over Scotty’s shoulder and glanced at the door plate, which read:

SCP-239

“So this isn’t a magic school after all.”  
The girl felt relieved but suddenly sad.  
…Perhaps her childhood had just vanished at this moment.

* * *

“Don’t move around, I’m using healing magic to restore your body.”  
In the RV the two had just arrived in, Scotty flipped through messages on her phone while using a strange technique to help Siglos, who was lying on the bed, regain mobility.

“You’ve been lying down too long; muscle atrophy is normal—though as a current… plastic—uh, better to call you a reality bender.”  
Scotty tossed her phone onto the nearby sofa in frustration.  
“As a reality bender, what’s more important is… hmm, your belief is your magic. So you have to firmly believe you can move normally to recover from this unfortunate state.”  
Siglos said nothing, only smiling and nodding. She slowly got up from the bed, took a copy of The Little Prince, and leaned against the headboard to read.  
Then she glanced at Scotty, curious about what kind of person this “prince” who had taken her out of the monotonous ‘white room’ really was, only to find that this young woman, who looked only slightly older than herself, had somehow pulled out a wooden guitar and was preparing to play.

“Is that a guitar…? My master likes to play one smaller than this.”  
“Ah… that’s a ukulele.”  

Scotty sighed and corrected her, then tuned the strings.  
She looked up and asked Siglos, “What do you want to hear?”  
Siglos thought for a moment and made a fairly simple request:

“I want… to hear something I’ve never heard before!”  
“Of course, no problem.”  

Scotty strummed the strings and began playing an unplugged vaporwave piece.  
Listening to the dreamy, ethereal melody, Siglos gradually drifted off to sleep.  
“Looks like she’s really not a bad person…”

* * *

The girl opened her eyes, awakening in this world that was once a paradise.  
She gazed at the sky that had long since changed: the sun no longer emitted dazzling endless light but hid behind clouds, showing the girl a twilight orange-red; the meadow still felt the breeze, but it carried the warmth of sunlight; no longer were there bubbles floating in the air, but if the girl wished, she could still conjure one or two as toys.

Isn’t it beautiful?

…Perhaps it is.  
“Much better than unchanging light!”  
The girl laughed freely in her dream.
