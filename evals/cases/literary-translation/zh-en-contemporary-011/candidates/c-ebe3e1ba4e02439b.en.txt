April 1, 2020, Clear  
At 191.4 direction, 4█ kilometers from ███ City, Xinjiang

2:41 PM

Just another ordinary day. I finished my routine report as usual. The climate of the Gobi Desert is really unsuitable for activity at noon. I’ve learned to find a place to conserve energy at midday, upload the routine report to the station server, and then set off again in the afternoon and evening to continue my journey. Although it’s not yet midsummer, the sun in the sky is already glaringly bright, almost hurting my eyes. I drove a few more kilometers, found a shady spot, set up the protective net stored in the trunk, and planned to take a short nap to relieve the fatigue from continuous driving.

Before sleeping, I took out my gun, disassembled the receiver and barrel, and carefully wiped the barrel to remove the windblown sand that had gotten inside the gun. The Type 95-1 is still very reliable, but I often maintain it anyway, simply because I have nothing else to do.

“When will days like this ever end?” I muttered softly while doing maintenance, complaining, “No girlfriend, no money, stuck in the Gobi Desert every day, and constantly having to deal with anomalies risking my life. I really want to go home. God, when will this ever end?”

I’ve been here for more than two years now. Even the border guards have rotated through once, but I still haven’t been transferred. I’m still doing my lone-wolf reconnaissance work. The basic investigation teams from the 19th Bureau and the Foundation usually have at least two people, but for some reason, our reconnaissance teams usually consist of just one person with one gun.

“God... I really can’t take it anymore...” I muttered to myself.

The only response was the fierce wind blowing across the Gobi. I leaned tightly against the car body to prevent the disassembled gun parts from being swept up by the fine gravel.

“████” I thought I heard some sound carried by the wind. It sounded almost like someone speaking.

I stopped what I was doing and pricked up my ears, carefully searching for the source of the sound... My nerves tightened. I put down my tools and slowly lifted the hem of my clothes, feeling the solid and cold grip of my Type 92 pistol.

My gaze swept across the boundless Gobi, but to no avail.

The EVE indicator showed no abnormal fluctuations. Holding it, I scanned around; the anomaly pointer stayed at 0.1 — my indicator has a small glitch where its baseline pointer is a bit higher than others. I’ve checked it several times but don’t know why. Anyway, it doesn’t affect my use, so I’m too lazy to replace it or even recalibrate it — recalibration requires sending it to the EVE particle shielding room for leveling. I went once before; it took a long time to finish, but it broke again shortly after. Since it doesn’t affect its function and it’s convenient to use, I haven’t bothered with anything else.

“Forget it, it’s probably just nerves.”

This kind of thing has often happened ever since I started patrolling and scouting here with my rifle. After all, it’s just the nerves caused by the desolate and uninhabited Gobi Desert, I thought to myself with a self-mocking shake of my head. I reassembled the gun, held it in my arms, and lay down to sleep.
