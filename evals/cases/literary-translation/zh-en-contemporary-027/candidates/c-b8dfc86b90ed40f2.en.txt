Moser returned to the site, dejectedly walked into the armory, took off his full-body protective suit, and put down the containment tools and firearms.

Today's containment operation had once again ended in failure. It wasn't because their squad lacked skill, nor because their containment plan had flaws. It was just that the overly "enthusiastic" crowd during the operation had interfered, resulting in the deaths of several civilians and injuries to more than a dozen others. Although no one in the squad died, there were some casualties.

He sat down on a chair in the armory, leaned against the backrest, and looked up at the big white light bulb in the room, as if there was some solution there to fix the failure of this containment operation.

Ever since the memory deletion technology failed due to an SCP incident five years ago, the Foundation had gradually stepped out of the shadows of the world. Their Mobile Task Forces (MTFs) began to be recognized by people, though this was limited to their work.

"Moser, you need to go apologize," a team member knocked on the armory door, bringing Moser back from his daydream.

Moser nodded, reached for his mask beside him, and walked out of the armory, heading straight toward the press conference.

The press conference was held inside the site. In a world like this, the Foundation had no secrets at all.

"Alpha-01, regarding this containment operation that caused 5 civilian deaths and 17 injuries, what are your thoughts?" A reporter saw Moser wearing his mask step onto the stage and immediately asked loudly, his voice filled with excitement at the prospect of a headline, showing no fear of the SCPs or sympathy for the injured civilians.

"...I am very sorry," Moser bowed and then said.

This kind of situation had happened so many times that Moser could write a speech for other MTF squad leaders, and they could do the same for him—writing speeches for everyone.

In the past, during containment operations, no ordinary person would come to help, let alone throw a cup of milk tea at an SCP. Usually, civilians would stay far away, government departments would help guide the crowd, and then they would lure the SCP to the outskirts or uninhabited areas for containment. The most troublesome part was not the MTFs but the memory deletion teams.

But now, everything had changed. They had gone from shadows into the light. SCPs had become items that could be legally traded on the black market, and some auction houses even auctioned SCPs. The Foundation was exposed to everyone. Governments around the world, leveraging the public's trust in their countries, pressured the Foundation, trying to obtain its technology.

[Everything has changed. We are no longer unknown, but we are no longer heroes either.] Moser thought as he looked at the reporters below, eager to ask questions.

"Alpha-01, why has the Foundation still not agreed to the United Nations' resolution to hand over SCPs to the five permanent members for management? Does the Foundation consider itself above the UN and the law?" This reflected distrust and misunderstanding of the Foundation.

"Alpha-01, can you tell us how many teams like yours the Foundation has? Do you get paid? Where does your operational funding come from?" This was to satisfy public curiosity.

"Alpha-01, every time you conduct a containment operation, there is a certain amount of property damage and casualties. Why not cooperate with the government and the people? That would reduce a lot of losses." This was a very idealistic suggestion.

...

Moser looked at the "Protect" icon on the site map and then at the crowded reporters below, suddenly feeling a surge of irritation.

[Is this what we are supposed to protect?] he thought.
