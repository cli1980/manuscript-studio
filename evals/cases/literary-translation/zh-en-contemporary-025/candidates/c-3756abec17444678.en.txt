You are the director of the regulatory bureau. The night is deep, yet not late; outside the window, bustling vehicles drag long beams of light as they pass by. You shake the glass in your hand; the ice cubes clink against the walls, slowly melting into the Lagavulin 1995 whiskey. You love this sound, and you deeply enjoy the tranquility of the night. In just a few days, your long-awaited vacation will begin. You have already planned the itinerary: Italy, Cinque Terre, the seaside of Monterosso, and a seafood feast prepared by Chef Marino for you and your wife. You imagine yourself, full and satisfied, burying your toes in the pure, warm sand, feeling the gentle breeze of the Mediterranean. It’s perfect—you can hardly wait.

“Honey, I’m going to bed. Do you want to come up?” your wife calls softly from upstairs.

“Okay, darling. I’ll be up soon,” you say, raising your glass to her. She smiles charmingly and turns to walk into the bedroom.

You want to stay a little longer, to fully enjoy the peaceful night. After all, today wasn’t exactly calm—you read many documents, signed many papers, and had a dinner with another director that was neither terrible nor exactly pleasant. Tomorrow, you’ll have to read even more documents and sign even more papers. You’re also planning to flirt a bit with the new secretary; that body tightly wrapped in a suit skirt is truly intoxicating. You yawn slightly as the alcohol begins to take effect. It’s about time to sleep—you really hope you’ll have a dream about Italy.

Oh, one more thing. You almost forgot. You put down the whiskey and take a small bottle from the drawer by the fireplace. You pour out a pale yellow pill and swallow it with the whiskey.

To be honest, you don’t like the pharmacist who sells this medicine—he’s eccentric and speaks strangely. But his medicine works. While you sleep, half of the pill’s ingredients corrode your stomach, drilling millions of holes, causing the “cancer” to disappear as it struggles to survive. The other half gently numbs your brain, granting you a tender pleasure that makes you feel nothing at all. At least, that’s what the pharmacist says. You’ve never looked into the exact ingredients. Judging by the current effect, you choose to believe him.

Of course, the medicine isn’t cheap, but you’ve never worried about money. Who are you? You’re the director. As long as you hold this position, there will always be people bringing you money, hoping you’ll sometimes turn a blind eye to certain matters. When was the last time? Something about the railway fence? You don’t remember, and you don’t care. In this era where people don’t easily die, a little corruption won’t cause much harm. Why bother?

You finish the last sip of whiskey, set down the glass, and head upstairs to the bedroom. So what if it’s cancer? It doesn’t disturb the beautiful world around you.
