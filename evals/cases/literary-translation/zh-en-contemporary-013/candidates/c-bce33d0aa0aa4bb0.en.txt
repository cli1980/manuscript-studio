I once thought that as time passed, Shiina Saeko and the magical girl Moonstone would, like other events happening in the same time slot, be torn apart by time, fading in color and blending into a sticky, featureless gelatinous mass. Even if I wanted to recall something, it would only be like pinching out a murky daily slice representing "childhood," gradually corrected by the brain into a single symbol, thus disappearing from my impression.

Until a few days ago, when a senior colleague from the same company dragged me to what was probably an impromptu drinking party.

After downing several large mugs of draft beer in succession, the tipsy drunks around the table naturally began to break the shackles of social rules. Among them was a heavily made-up woman wearing a low-cut blouse and skirt, who, after getting drunk, simply tore open her collar, revealing lace-trimmed underwear and hastily smeared tattoo scars on her collarbone. She slurred as she boasted to others about her "feats" as a member of a delinquent student gang in middle school—I originally intended to just muddle through among these drunks by relying on squid tentacles, but soon, even the last bit of my appetite disappeared.

As if a silent sneer echoed in my ear, the words and names she uttered were unusually familiar, flooding back along with memories perhaps deliberately glossed over in my subconscious. An inexplicable surge of fear burst from my heart, making me want to find an excuse to leave temporarily, but the already drunken senior grabbed me firmly and, without a word, pressed me back into my seat and forced a few shots of soju down my throat.

The burning sensation of the alcohol suddenly surged to my forehead. I can no longer recall what happened after enduring those fragmented stories that seemed to piece together some outline like torture. I only remember finally staggering back to my apartment, even inserting the key into the lock—a small task—took quite some effort. The next blackout was me stumbling into the bathtub overflowing with water, lifting my head past the dripping wet bangs to look at myself in the bathroom mirror, covered in mist. At some unknown time—perhaps an illusion—an irregular bulge appeared on my chest, pushing the skin above into a slight curve. My trembling fingertips brushed over it, feeling cold and hard.

...just like a moonstone that has lost its shine.

Thinking carefully, perhaps I truly lack the qualifications to become a magical girl. No alien creature with whom I made a contract, no battle suit, no ability to release colorful magic, no magic wand, cards, cannons, or even burning bows and guns to fight the monsters crawling in the shadows. I didn't even notice the darkness that Shiina-san, who was close by, was facing. I just grew up quietly, unnoticed, and silent into a mediocre and cowardly adult.

—but maybe sometimes, a few sleeping pills mixed into draft beer, a well-sharpened bone cleaver, and a claw hammer taped firmly to the right hand can belatedly answer anyone's prayers. Now, I sincerely believe this.
