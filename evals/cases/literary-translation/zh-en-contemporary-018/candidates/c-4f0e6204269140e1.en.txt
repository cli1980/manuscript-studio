Ever since I came to the big city, I’ve felt uncomfortable all over. Every day I have to carefully budget for rent, utilities, and daily necessities like rice, oil, and salt. Back home, living on three thousand yuan a month was never this tight, yet my parents still brag to relatives and friends that I earn over ten thousand a month. That’s true, but I can’t save a single penny. Looking over my accounts, I always feel like I’m wasting a lot of money, but when I think it through again, without these little indulgences, I really couldn’t survive. I guess I’m a coward. I remember Camel Xiangzi—he was so miserable yet still went to the White House and bought cigarettes. I used to laugh at him when I was a kid.

Today, my boss dragged me around again to clean up his mess. When I left the office building, I looked up and saw the moon. But the streetlights were still on and wouldn’t go off, not even during the day. This city doesn’t allow sleep, but tonight was really excessive. All the billboards were covered with ads for some rat poison product—a huge dead rat with a bizarre bright red background that looked like someone had set the RGB to 255,0,0. Shameless. I walked toward the subway entrance wearing a heavy red uniform that I couldn’t take off because the boss said we can’t remove it during work hours. This is what happens when a psychopath is in charge.

The elevator at the subway entrance was shut down due to power restrictions, so I took the stairs step by step. Was the air conditioning off too because of the power limit? Why was the empty subway station so hot? I suspected it was the heat inside my head because the station was also taken over by the rat poison ads. Bright red and dead rats covered the walls and gates. When I got on the train, the windows outside were all bright red too.

Having money means doing whatever you want. So be it.

* * *

Today, another overtime shift. People on the street were all looking up—turns out there was a once-in-a-century red moon tonight. But what does that have to do with me? Maybe only the local rich kids have the leisure to care about that. The buildings along the street were still plastered with bright red and dead rats, even more aggressively than before. Between the shop doors and windows were ads everywhere, invading every corner, so much so that the red glow even tinted the street lamps, casting light over the dead rats scattered on the ground.

The subway station was oppressively hot, making me worry I was getting sick. Now the ads weren’t satisfied with just a few signs—they brazenly invaded the floor, somehow glowing bright red. Their background wasn’t just bright red anymore; it was covered with neatly and densely arranged large characters that read:

Kill kill kill kill kill kill kill kill kill kill kill kill kill kill kill kill kill

Die die die die die die die die die die die die die die die die die

Exterminate exterminate exterminate exterminate exterminate exterminate exterminate exterminate exterminate exterminate exterminate exterminate exterminate exterminate exterminate exterminate

Kids like it, families feel safe, just use Yi Saoguang® Strong Rat Exterminator!

The characters stretched far away, forming stripes covered with dead rats. I felt like I was stepping on something, even touching something on the handrails, so I avoided those dead rats and staggered down the stairs like a drunk, walking in a strange, wobbly way. The seats and handrails on the subway were also covered with dead rats, so I had to stride over them and stand like a model. Suddenly the train accelerated, throwing me to the ground. Immediately I heard the sounds of crushing, breaking, and... people screaming.

That night I showered for over an hour, almost late for work the next day.
