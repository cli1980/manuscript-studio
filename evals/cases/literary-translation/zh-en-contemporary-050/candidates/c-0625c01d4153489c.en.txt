Dinner time------

“We are like gods. This is simply an anomaly.”

“Yes. If you are a god, then you are what you are. As for why you are that way, that’s another matter. Many theories have been proposed about this. But no matter what, we have always been protecting the world with this, and it works well.”

“But this seems... too easy. So the Foundation’s work is just that simple, just give a rating? Other organizations just let us manipulate them?”

“In a sense, yes, but not entirely. Not all Keter-class anomalies are that simple and violent; it’s just that most anomalies that qualify as Keter are simple and violent.” The superior took a bite of noodles, then started giving an extracurricular orientation for newcomers: “But that’s only most of them. There are still some threats—some monsters that will kill you, or things that will drive you insane—dangerous and fascinating.”

“Are there any standards for evaluation? Can we use this for something?”

“No, basically not. If you insist there is a standard, it’s ‘you think it’s good.’ It’s like a judge’s free discretion; there’s no way to set an absolutely accurate standard, and even if you did, it wouldn’t help. The committee’s biased judgments are meaningless; it was established just as an administrative convenience. What really works is the evaluations from all Foundation insiders who know about the anomaly. We just divide and concentrate the work. If the committee rates SCP-682 as unqualified, but still can’t kill it—I don’t know if that’s part of its ability, but to most staff, although it’s fierce and dangerous, it’s indeed very... special.”

The superior took a sip of tea. “Similarly, sometimes we do want to keep certain SCPs, but they themselves are very uninteresting. An infinite energy perpetual motion machine, a fountain of youth that reverses aging, a panacea that cures all diseases—their value is self-evident, but as anomalies, they’re like children’s ideas; to my most strict judging colleagues, they’re downright disgusting.”

“SCP-500 is still there, and is listed as ‘Legacy.’”

“That’s the subtle part. We can’t force everyone to consider interesting things as uninteresting, but making something interesting—at least enough to move the judges observing it—is quite easy, like adding one or two deeply touching story appendices. When the committee secretly initiated the legacy evaluation procedure for SCP-500, Agent A.A and Iris had just single-handedly dealt with two Keter-class anomalies, at the cost of becoming inhuman. So the O5 first cooperated by playing the ‘white face’ role, giving a deeply moving speech, plus posthumously awarding the Foundation Star in advance, telling them to wait for death; then the ‘red face’ team members ‘privately’ used two doses of the panacea, followed by intense debates within the Ethics Committee and even among the O5s. Such a dramatic story was enough.”

T.D was somewhat incredulous. He knew about A.A and Iris’s deeds of fighting off Keter-class attacks to execute their insane captain—they were heroes—but he didn’t know the Foundation could exploit its own heroes like this.

“Believe it or not.” The superior smiled.
