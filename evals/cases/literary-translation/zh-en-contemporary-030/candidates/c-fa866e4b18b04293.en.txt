When the plane was landing, it was raining outside. She propped her face on her hand and looked out the window, just waking from a restless nap. When she opened the shade, all she saw was a white haze of clouds and the barren plains covered beneath them. She felt an inexplicable anxiety, as if she always involuntarily got nervous whenever something big was about to happen. She looked to the right; he was reading the travel guide provided on the plane. She held his hand, thinking it was like coffee going down her throat.

He withdrew his gaze and looked at her, then at the land behind her. "The plane was delayed by an hour. How do you feel?" he asked.

"Hard to say, a bit nauseous," she said. "And nervous." She looked out the window again.

"It's okay," he stroked her hand. "Nothing to worry about."

"I know, it's just... this is a decision, you know?" She seemed frustrated as she patted his hand. "Like a new phase or something."

"But it's probably mostly psychological," she added.

He just listened.

"By the way, do people really come to Yellowstone for tourism?" she finally asked.

"I don't know, probably," he said. Then they both fell silent. For some reason, the rumble and roar of the engines made her feel at ease.

When the landing chime sounded, the temperature had somehow dropped very low. The flight attendant's voice sounded like it was coming through a storm. She wrapped her clothes tighter, turned off the air conditioning, and wondered what the climate outside the plane would be like—how it would feel on her skin, the breath. She watched the plane slide along the wet runway, then come to a stop. When the flight attendant's voice sounded again, instructing people to disembark in an orderly manner, it sounded even more desperate than before.

It was raining outside, but there was still a dry feeling in the air. She took a deep breath, and a relieving coolness spread through her body. She felt her heartbeat heavy.

They took a Yellowstone bus to the hotel. The bus drove through a valley; the road conditions were not very good. She felt a bit carsick, so she leaned against the window to get some fresh air, her hair drifting along her cheek. She saw many trees, but most of the leaves were somewhat yellowed. After a while, they left the valley and came to a plain. In the distance stood a solitary gray building. As they approached, she saw it had a special saddle shape, which made her feel at ease. "Uterus," she said.

"I've always wanted to ask, why is it called the Uterus?" he said.

"The uterus is an organ people used long ago for reproduction. Now it has degenerated," she answered. "It's inside my body, but it's useless; it can't nurture life."

"But tomorrow," she said.

"Tomorrow," he said.

By the time they arrived, it was completely dark.

They took showers, then lay on the bed. The hotel was nice, feeling like staying in a big wooden cabin. The sheets were very white, without a single stain.

They turned off the lights, leaving only a small warm-colored lamp by the bedside, making the room very dim.

After a while, they started making love, then cuddled and caressed each other.

Then she said, "Should we be doing this?" She wanted to say more but stopped.

He was stunned for a moment, then asked, "Which parts?"

She said nothing.
