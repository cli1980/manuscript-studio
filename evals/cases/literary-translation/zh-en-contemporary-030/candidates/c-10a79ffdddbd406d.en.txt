It was raining when the plane landed. With a hand propping up her cheek, she gazed out the window, just waking from a brief, restless nap. As she opened the shade, she could only see a cloud of white dust and the desolate plain beneath it. She felt an inexplicable anxiety, as if she couldn't help tensing up whenever something significant was about to happen. She looked to her right. He was reading the travel guide provided by the airline. She held his hand. Just like coffe going down my throat, she thought.

He looked away and turned back to her, then to the land behind her. "The flight's delayed by one hour. How do you feel now?" He asked.

"Hard to tell. A bit nauseous," she said, "and nervous." She looked back at the window.

"It's okay." He stroked her hand. "Nothing to worry about."

"I know, it's just that… it's a decision, you know?" She patted his hand as if in frustration. "Like a new stage or something."

"But maybe I'm just overthinking." She added.

He just listened.

"By the way, does anyone actually come to Yellostone as a tourist?" She asked finally.

"Dunno. Maybe." He said. Then they both fell silent. The vibration and rumble of the engines somehow put her at ease.

The temperature had somehow dropped significantly when the landing announcement rang out. The flight attendant's voice sounded as if they were in a storm. She wrapped her coat tightly around herself, turned off the air conditioning, and wondered what the weather would be like outside the plane and what it would feel like on her skin. She breathed and watched as the plane taxied down the wet runway before stopping. The flight attendant sounded even more desperate when instructing people to disembark in an orderly manner.

There was driness in the air, though it was raining. She took a deep breath, and a cool sense of relief spread through her body. She could feel her heavy heartbeat.

They took the Yellowstone bus to the hotel. The bus drove through the valley. The road conditions weren't great. She felt a bit carsick, so she leaned against the window to get some air, hair fluttering along her cheeks. She saw many trees, though most of the leaves were a bit yellowed. They left the valley after a while. Ahead lay a stretch of plain, and in the distance stood a solitary gray building. As they drew closer, she saw that it had a distinctive saddle-like shape, which put her at ease. "The Womb," she said.

"I've always wondered, why is it called The Womb?" He said.

"The womb is an organ people used for reproduction a long time ago, but it has since become vestigial." She replied. "There's one in my body. But it's useless. It cannot give birth to life."

"But tomorrow," she said.

Tomorrow, he said.

It was completely dark when they arrived.

They took showers and lay on the bed. It was a nice hotel and felt like living in a large hut. The sheet was white and spotless.

They turned off the lights, leaving only a small, warm-colored lamp by the bed. The room was dim.

They had sex after a while. Then they cuddled up together and caressed each other.

Then she said: "Should we do that?" She had meant to say more, but she stopped.

He paused for a moment, then asked: "Do what?"

She didn't reply.
