Foundation, Site-17, Headquarters of Project Illusion®

UTC-5, August 15, 2016, 9 PM

“Now we can move the plan to the next step, Fanta-chan.” The figure of O5-3 appeared simultaneously on several large screens. Coupled with her highly technological girl outfit and slightly mechanical-sounding voice, the scene looked very much like a reenactment from some sci-fi anime.

Fanta.AIC has a prototype body that looks fairly human-like—not because the Foundation can’t make 3D beautiful girl robots, but because AIAD gave AIC’s appearance a somewhat wild beauty (to the extent that at last year’s AIC collective meeting, the scene was often referred to by Foundation personnel as the “Humanoid Constellation Shining Moment”). This was mainly due to the Ethics Committee’s requirements; they did not want AI to look more human than humans.

“Yes, Overseer No. 3. But according to your plan, we need the assistance of Overseer No. 13, as well as permissions from other AICs. More dangerously, there is the sensitivity of conducting operations outside the Curtain.”

“You know how many times we’ve bypassed the Ethics Committee, Fanta-chan. Besides, I don’t need to appear personally all the time. Debuting outside the Curtain only requires one idol-style AIC prototype, then just modify her character design accordingly.”

It was obvious that after watching dozens of idol anime, O5-3 had fully adapted to speaking this way. There is a so-called uncanny valley theory in the two-dimensional world: if your speech and your two-dimensional appearance don’t match, it can be extremely awkward. But Overseer No. 3 generally maxes out both, making the live broadcast almost indistinguishable from an idol anime.

“But... will the people outside the Curtain accept you doing this? This form is somewhat...”

“Don’t you get it yet, Fanta-chan?” O5-3 turned around. “They can’t not accept it. Want to know why?”

Silence.

“Excellent hunters often appear in the form of their prey, while excellent traps often disdain concealment.” Her figure disappeared from the screen. “A truly clever trap is like this: even if you know full well that what’s in front of you is a trap, you still fall for it 100%. And Project Illusion is exactly such a trap.”

“They all know that everything before their eyes is an illusion, that the wealth they spend will bring no real benefit. So what happens? The gift rankings still change every day.”

“But if you put it that way, wouldn’t that mean...”

“What else? Not just AISaraChan and Project Illusion. All of this,” Overseer No. 3 appeared again, drawing a circle that almost filled the entire screen.

“Is a trap.”

After a brief silence, O5-3 spoke again: “Why do so many people accept absurd internet celebrities and idol dramas with disgusting plots? Or rather, why do so many people become addicted to completely fictional things and are willing to spend their wealth on them? Especially after the year 2000, when the internet rose?”

“The principle is simple. To put it plainly, what you consume represents your identity. Through this process of identity classification, plundering the wealth of these groups is as easy as pie.”

“Decades ago, this still required some economic experts to devise slow methods to alienate groups—but today, especially on the internet, thanks to its powerful connectivity, this classification is almost zero difficulty.”

“As for the people outside the Curtain, even GoIs inside the Curtain cannot recognize the trap of AISaraChan, a thoroughly virtual character. How can you expect those outside the Curtain to resist these temptations?”

Fanta.AIC had nothing to say.

“Understood. I have requested the prototype AIC to enter the development process. Once the settings are complete, we can begin the next step.”
