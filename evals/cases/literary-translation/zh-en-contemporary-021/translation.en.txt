It was last May. I was going to perform a heart transplant. The patient was a 12-year-old kid. Poor little thing with congenital heart disease. Fortunate — fortunately, there was a donor heart that happened to be a perfect match for him. The surgery went smoothly, and there was no significant rejection afterward. At the time, it was the smoothest heart transplant I had ever performed in my entire career.

But then… then, the donor's family would like to visit the patient; they wanted to see the child whose life had been saved by their child's still-beating heart. This was prohibited actually. Do you know about the double-blind principle in organ donation? The donor and recipient are not allowed to know each other's information — supposedly to reduce the psychological burden on the recipient and prevent organ trafficking. But… but the donor's family was also in a terrible situation. Their son was in a young age when he had an accident and ended up in a vegetative state; now, all they wanted was to see him one last time, just to have something to hold onto. I… I talked with the recipient's family, and they felt sympathy for the other side as well and agreed. So I broke the rules and told them where the kid was. I thought it was just to let them see him one last time…
