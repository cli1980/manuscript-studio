In the damp, cold tunnels, even sound was a luxury. Frank struggled to move his body, trying to banish the chill, but his head immediately scraped against a rock wall. The hard, rough sensation was unpleasant, and before he could catch his breath or soothe his numbed nerves, instinct was already urging him to turn around in the cramped space and begin the day's work. For Frank, this was just another ordinary day in June.

He tentatively touched the rock layer with his forehead, then quickly withdrew as if burned. He leaned in again, pressing his lips to the wall. His overly protruding eyeballs brought a slight sting as they moved stiffly. The cold made him uncomfortable, as if he were close to an iceberg, but he didn't care much. From the depths of the silent darkness came faint scraping sounds—something gnawing on stone.

Crunch, crunch.

Crunch, crunch. Gurgle.

Frank gnawed at the rock with his pale, sharp, jagged teeth. The fragments crumbled between his tongue and palate, tumbling down into his stomach. He didn't remember where he was or what he was doing. He was just eating. In the darkness, countless eyes turned stiffly, their pupils faded. Countless scraping sounds, rhythmless and disjointed, but monotonous. Countless sharp teeth grinding against rock—more scraping sounds, like the simultaneous operation of an entire city's excavators.

Crunch, crunch. Gurgle.

Frank and his kin's lips never stopped moving. Their mouths seemed like bottomless pits that could devour everything. Minerals and metals rolled into their digestive tracts, coated in fluids, broken down and absorbed, flowing into the tips of their blood vessels and into their eternally cold bodies, becoming new nutrients. At the same time, harmful heavy metals and toxins accumulated in their bodies, but no one cared, no one.

Crunch, crunch.

Crunch, crunch.

Frank kept eating.

More stones disappeared, reduced to countless fine particles of dust in the guts of the pioneers—oh, no one likes those hard stones. No matter how hard you try, you get nowhere. They are cold, stubborn, always blocking others' paths, utterly tiresome.

Then came news. Radio waves spread in a way no one could perceive, bringing ominous tidings. The watchers had foreseen the great feat in the darkness and tried to stop and destroy it. The retreat was sealed off, the only light was stolen from the underground world, but Frank did not stop. He kept eating.

Gurgle, gurgle.

Crunch.

Creak.

Crunch.

Creak.

Frank devoured everything.

Suddenly, a vibration came from afar, gentle at first, very soft. But even Frank, who was naturally solitary and accustomed to nature's strange signs, felt deep unease. The scraping sounds in the darkness stopped all at once. Thousands of pairs of pale, glassy eyes turned in unison—left, right, upward.

If anyone had ever experienced such a scene, it would affect every second of the rest of their lives. Whenever they closed their eyes, they would hear the sound of the world shattering into pieces. Those gray, stiff eyes that brought and welcomed death would fill every corner of their dreams. Of course, that's assuming they survived the disaster.

Large chunks of rock fell away. The cave began to tremble violently. Terrifying roars echoed continuously, and indescribable things moved in maddening trajectories. Mountains fractured, tsunamis and earthquakes followed. Magma erupted, falling upon cities. The collapsing crust of the earth fluttered like a sail in a storm, torn to shreds by unstoppable forces. Flickering stars plunged into the bottomless sea. Layers of fish carcasses accumulated beneath the ruins. Lifeless debris drifted and scattered across the empty ocean surface.

Frank stopped eating.

But human civilization had already been destroyed in an instant.
