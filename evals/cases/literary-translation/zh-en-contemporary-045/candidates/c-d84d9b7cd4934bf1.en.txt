In the damp, cold tunnel, even a breath was a luxury. Frank struggled to move his body, trying to drive the chill out, but then his head bumped against the stone wall beside him. The hard, rough texture made him uncomfortable, but before he could catch his breath and ease the slight numbness from the impact, instinct urged him to turn around in the cramped space and throw himself into today's work. For Frank, this was just another ordinary day in June.

He tested the rock layer with his forehead, then quickly pulled back as if burned by fire. Then he leaned in again, pressing his lips against the rock wall. The overly protruding eyeballs rotated stiffly, causing a slight stinging sensation. The cold made him feel miserable, as if he were close to an iceberg, but he didn't care much. From the depths of the silent darkness came faint scratching sounds—something was gnawing at the stone.

Creak, creak.

Creak, creak. Gurgle.

Frank bit into the rock layer with his pale, sharp, serrated teeth. The fragments that fell apart crumbled between his tongue and mouth, tumbling down into his stomach. He didn't remember where he was, nor did he know what exactly he was doing; he was just eating. In the darkness, countless pairs of eyes rotated stiffly, their pupils faded. Countless scratching sounds, without rhythm or coherence, but all the same. Countless sharp teeth grinding against the rock, more scratching sounds, as if the entire city's excavators had started simultaneously.

Creak, creak. Gurgle.

Frank and his kin's lips opened and closed continuously, their mouths like bottomless pits that could devour everything. Minerals and metals were swallowed into their digestive tracts, wrapped in juices, broken down and absorbed, flowing into the ends of their blood vessels, coursing through their eternally cold bodies, becoming new nutrients. Likewise, harmful heavy metals and toxins accumulated in their bodies, but no one cared, no one.

Creak, creak.
Creak, creak.

Frank kept eating.

More stones disappeared, turning into countless tiny dust particles that vanished in the pioneers' intestines—oh, no one liked those hard stones; no matter how hard you tried, you would gain nothing in front of them. They were indifferent, stubborn, always blocking the way, utterly annoying.

News arrived; radio signals spread in ways no one could perceive, bringing ominous messages. The watchers foresaw the feats in the dark depths and tried to stop and sabotage them. The retreat was sealed off, the only light was taken away from the underground world, yet Frank did not stop; he kept eating.

Gurgle, gurgle.

Creak.

Squeak.

Creak.

Squeak.

Frank devoured everything.

Suddenly, a distant vibration came, at first gentle and mild. Even Frank, naturally reclusive and long accustomed to the strange phenomena of nature, felt profound unease. The gnawing sounds in the darkness stopped instantly; thousands of pale, glassy eyes turned simultaneously—to the left—to the right—upward.

If someone had ever experienced this scene, it would affect every second of their life thereafter. Every time they closed their eyes, they would hear the sound of the world collapsing and shattering. Those gray-white, stiff eyeballs, bringing and simultaneously welcoming death, would fill every corner of their dreams. Of course, that was if they survived this catastrophe.

Large chunks of rock fell off, the cave began to tremble violently, terrifying roars echoed continuously, indescribable beings moved along frenzied trajectories. Mountains cracked, tsunamis and earthquakes followed, lava spewed out, falling into the city. The collapsing crust was like a sail in a storm, torn to pieces by an unstoppable force; flickering stars fell into the bottomless deep sea. Layers of fish corpses accumulated beneath the ruins; lifeless dead things scattered across the empty sea surface.

Frank no longer ate.

But human civilization was destroyed in the blink of an eye.
