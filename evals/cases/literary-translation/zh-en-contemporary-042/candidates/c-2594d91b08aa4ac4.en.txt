Next stop, Lam Tin...

From the next station onward, the remaining journey is spent entirely inside tunnels. As the scenery outside the window quickly fades into pitch black, the bright and polished interior of the carriage also fades away, revealing the rusty, blood-stained, and dilapidated appearance beneath. The floor creaks underfoot, and when I lift my foot, a sticky trail of flesh and blood stretches out.

The train tilts downward, heading toward the underground station. In the distance, I see a passenger accidentally fall to the ground and silently merge into the flesh and blood of the floor. Instinctively, I grab the handrail beside the bench—the words of the bull-headed fellow were indeed reliable.

After the train stops, floodwaters surge into the carriage through every door, washing the floor with water up to half a calf high. But at the same time, a group of bloated passengers are brought in. Each of them is swollen like a balloon, emitting waves of putrid stench that make my head spin. I’m sure the sight of these “balloons” bursting would be too grotesque to look at.

I really want to get off, but outside the train is a vast swamp with nowhere to step—so the bull-headed fellow was truly reliable; there’s no turning back now.

* * *

Next stop, Yau Tong...

The train continues downward, and the temperature inside the carriage gradually becomes scorching. The odors of the new passengers intensify under the heat, and combined with the oppressive temperature, my dazed mind can barely think.

This time, what enters through the open doors are burning fire people—rather than walking in, they crawl in. Not only is their own flesh burning, but they are also covered in hot oil.

One by one, the passengers from the previous station start to boil, then explode. Corrupt water and chunks of flesh fly everywhere, splashing onto the fire people and triggering even bigger explosions. Suddenly, the carriage is engulfed in flames, and I have nowhere to hide.

* * *

Next stop, Tiu Keng Leng...

The train has reached the terminal station. All passengers please disembark...

Then I wake up again. The train is smoothly pulling into the terminal station, and passengers around me are getting off. I also awaken from this strange nightmare.

There’s never enough sleep every day. The little sleep I get on the subway is just as precious. How could it be the freest time of the day? There’s no such thing.

I yawn, and the smell in my mouth feels familiar—like the odor emitted by those balloon people in the dream. When I stand up and step out of the carriage, the scene before my eyes freezes me in place—

It’s not a station platform, but a hill made of stairs. The overcast sky hangs with a series of nooses. The passengers who got off earlier each walk to a different noose, place it around their necks, and then the nooses tighten and rise. Their bodies twitch wildly before going still, turning into chunks of flesh flowing with yellowish-white substances.

These chunks of flesh hanging from the nooses form a forest of ropes on the stair hill.

I carefully avoid the filth on the ground, step aside from each “tree,” and climb to the top of the hill, gazing out at the endless rope forest.

A noose hangs right in front of me, at just the right height.

Well, there’s no turning back anymore. It’s better than going to work, I guess.

So I put the noose around my neck and ascend to the end of the journey.
