Later, in certain moments of his long life, he would also recall where it all began.

At that time, Bright had not yet earned his doctorate degree. To make it easier to distinguish, let's call him Jack.

At that time, Evelyn Bright was still a woman with a normal human appearance.

Jack's mother, like his father, was away from home for long periods, so much so that many years later he could only remember a beautiful but vague phantom. But no matter what, the attraction of "mother" was still immense. So on that afternoon when Evelyn called Jack away, TJ also clamored to stick close.

The woman held each of her sons by one hand, walking down the grayish town street. The older one felt uncomfortable for a while, eventually breaking free from her hand and silently following alongside. He was already a sensitive and awkward teenager, but his mother still seemed to treat him like a child.

Suddenly losing one side of warmth, Evelyn instinctively pulled TJ closer; the boy with reddish-brown curly hair looked up, then obediently curled up and leaned into her arms.

Jack watched coldly. Although TJ was the one initially sought out, he unknowingly received more attention—of course, mothers always favored the younger child. And Jack Bright, as the second son in the family, had always lived under eyes that held few demands or expectations, like an inconspicuous weed growing freely and boldly before anyone noticed.

Evelyn did not take the brothers far; the swelling of her abdomen made it difficult for her to stand for long. The group stopped at a snack stall at the street corner. The woman hesitated for a moment and, from the meager options, ordered a waffle each for the two children.

The early summer sunlight caused a slight sting on the skin at the back of Jack's neck. TJ, unable to suppress his childish nature, bit into the freshly baked treat and swayed idly beside him.

But Jack secretly straightened his back. Holding onto what he considered mature dignity, he stood motionless, side by side with his mother, both watching the slowly browning color emerging on the waffle iron grid.

During this long and silent wait, Evelyn's voice was like a rustling wind. Soft and sigh-like, it was blown apart and fragmented by the real wind.

"Mikell told me you want to apply for the biology major?"

His mother asked.

"Yes."

"Have you... really thought it through?"

Jack looked up; a dense fog of worry clouded his mother's deep green eyes. He didn't understand where this emotion came from and cautiously replied, "Yes. Is there a problem with biology?"

"...No." Evelyn raised her hand, as if to ruffle his hair, but instead it turned and rested on his shoulder. "I just didn't expect you to be going to college so soon, James. It's really fast."

"I skipped two grades," he reminded her.

"I know, your father mentioned it to me," she tilted her head to study him for a moment, then smiled slightly. "I originally wanted to talk to you about this carefully, but since you've decided... then go for it."

Many years later, Jack Bright would still recall that summer day. The wind carried floating dust, stirring the first moist scent before the rainy season arrived. The sky was vast, and the expression between the woman's brows was comforting and relaxed.

In the week that followed, the Bright family's youngest child, his sister Sarah, was born.

He never again saw his mother smile like that.
