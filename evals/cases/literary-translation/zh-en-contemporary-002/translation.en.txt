One

When the containment door opened, the cleaner Vodka froze.

The contained object before him, SCP-173—a grotesquely proportioned, mustard-yellow concrete head—had somehow acquired a freshly printed, still-warm photograph stuck to its surface.

17 by 13 centimeters, 21 inches diagonal, 5 megapixels, plenty sharp enough. The only question was whether it was black-and-white or color, since SCP-096's skin was very pale, and its face even paler.

No one knew how the photograph had been attached.

The print was new, the ink still wet, meaning it had been put there only moments ago. Yet 173 had been perfectly fine up until yesterday. During that time, no one had entered the containment chamber, the cameras had recorded nothing, and no other sensors had registered the slightest trace… Everything was too perfect, too clean to be the crime of any normal or reasonable being.

The only possibility was… an upper narrative had stealthily entered the containment chamber (after all, they need not abide by any normal or anomalous laws), pressed a freshly imagined photograph onto 173's head, then settled into a convenient narrative angle to watch how Vodka and the Foundation would tackle this "problem of the century."

Either way, Vodka's fate was sealed. Targeted by 096, he was now a corpse that could still speak, an indisputable fact. And faced with this absolute Apollyon, the Foundation had no way to stop such a match-made-in-heaven pairing of anomalous properties. Human civilization would be utterly destroyed by this terrifying anomaly.

"Uh…" Vodka stood still for a moment, then gently closed the door.

Right, he was a dead man, but 173 couldn't open doors. Just close the door and that was it.

He checked his watch. 096's containment site was over a dozen kilometers away. There was plenty of time to notify the containment team to revise the containment procedures: lock the door, seal it with poured concrete, turn off the cameras, and no more cleaning up the excrement.

No one would ever clean it again. The first thought that flashed through Vodka's mind was precisely that—give it a few years, and 173 would probably end up steeped into a soft sludge by its own excrement, filling half the containment chamber.

And by the time 096 arrived to tear him apart, he would have already settled his affairs and could lie comfortably in a coffin, waiting to die.

He would indeed be sacrificed, but the world, far from being destroyed, wouldn't have a damn thing wrong with it.

Two

The screen flickered, and in a dazed blink, Vodka found himself standing before the containment door once again.

What was going on…? Confused for a moment, he couldn't help but glance around him.

Standing beside him was a mustard-yellow concrete statue of a large head.

With the mindset that a Foundation employee should be ready to lay down his life, Vodka instinctively tried to haul 173 back into the containment chamber, but where the door had been was now nothing but cold, solid wall.

So that was it. As obvious as it was shameless, the upper narrative had cheated. It had forcibly started over.

It seemed unsatisfied that its design had been so easily foiled, so it had played a spiteful little trick, patched the loophole, and started again, ready to settle in and savor the sight of Vodka at his wit's end.

The situation stumped Vodka for a moment. Frowning, he fixed his eyes on 173's big head and that photograph, racking his brain with difficulty—and then, with utter decisiveness, he reached out, peeled the photograph off, and tore it to shreds.

Right, it was that simple. Within five minutes, Vodka had saved the world twice, without expending more brainpower than a five-year-old. The solution to this conundrum was so straightforward: find some poor scapegoat, and rip the photograph up.

Though in the end, he would still die at the hands of 096. But clearly, that little matter was of no consequence to the world.

—So wouldn't sending a D-class have worked just as well?
