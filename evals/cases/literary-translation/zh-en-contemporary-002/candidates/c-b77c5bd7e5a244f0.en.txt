One

When the containment chamber door opened, the cleaner Vodka was stunned.

Before him was the containment subject SCP-173—a disproportionate, earth-yellow concrete head—somehow had a freshly printed, still warm photo stuck onto it.

17x13 inches, 21 inches diagonal, 5 million pixels, clear enough, though it was unclear whether it was black and white or color, since SCP-096’s skin is very pale, and his face even paler.

No one knew how this photo got stuck there.

The photo was very new, the ink not dry, indicating it had just been put up. But until yesterday, 173 was fine, and no one had entered the containment chamber during this time. Cameras recorded nothing, and other sensors showed no traces... everything was too perfect, it shouldn’t be a crime committed by any normal or reasonable entity.

The only possibility was... some higher-level narrative forcibly infiltrated the containment chamber (after all, they don’t need to obey any normal or abnormal rules), took a freshly imagined photo and slapped it onto 173’s head, then found a good narrative angle to sit back and watch how Vodka and the Foundation would deal with this “problem of the century.”

In any case, Vodka’s fate was sealed. Being targeted by 096, he was a corpse that could speak—an indisputable fact. And facing this definite Apollyon-class threat, the Foundation couldn’t stop such a perfect match of anomalous properties. Human civilization would be utterly destroyed by this terrifying anomaly.

“Uh...” Vodka was stunned for a moment, then gently closed the door.

Right, he was doomed, but 173 wouldn’t open the door. Just closing it was enough.

He checked his watch; 096’s site was more than ten kilometers away. There was enough time to notify the containment team to change containment procedures: lock the door, pour concrete to seal it, turn off the cameras, and stop cleaning the excrement.

No one would clean anymore. The first thought crossing Vodka’s mind was this—after a few years, 173 would probably have soaked its half-filled containment chamber of excrement into soft mud.

And when 096 came to tear him apart, he would have already arranged his affairs and could lie comfortably in his coffin waiting to die.

He was indeed sacrificed, but the world was far from destroyed; nothing serious had happened.

Two

The screen flickered, and in a daze, Vodka found himself standing again in front of the containment chamber door.

What’s going on...? He was momentarily confused and involuntarily looked to his side.

Standing with him was a concrete head statue, earth-yellow in color.

With the mindset that Foundation staff should be willing to sacrifice themselves, Vodka instinctively wanted to carry 173 back into the containment chamber, but the place where the door had been was now a cold, hard wall.

So that’s it. Obviously and shamelessly, that higher-level narrative cheated; it forcibly restarted.

It seemed dissatisfied that its design was so easily cracked, so it played a dirty trick, patched the loophole, and started over, preparing to leisurely watch Vodka’s helplessness again.

This situation momentarily stumped Vodka. He frowned, staring intently at 173’s big head and the photo, thinking hard—then decisively reached out, peeled the photo off, and tore it to pieces.

Yes, it was that simple. Within five minutes, Vodka saved the world twice, without using more intelligence than a five-year-old child. The solution to this problem was so straightforward: find a scapegoat and tear up the photo.

Although in the end, he still had to die at 096’s hands. But obviously, this little matter had nothing to do with the world’s pain or gain.

—So if it’s that simple, why not just send a D-class?
