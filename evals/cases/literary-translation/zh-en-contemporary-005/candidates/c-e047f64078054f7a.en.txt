In the first few decades after becoming SCP-963, Dr. Jack Bright occasionally found it kind of fun.

He had worked with many people, all geniuses, eccentrics, lunatics, or whatever else unknown to the world. But Bright had to admit that although his colleagues were the farthest beings from what you might think of as "colleagues," he did not dislike the days spent with them—at least, most of the time.

Of course, things were not always so interesting later on: Clef disappeared without a trace, Kondraki went missing—there were rumors that Gears killed him, and Gears had become more and more like a real gear after Iceberg’s suicide; Kain was exiled to the lab to grow old alone, Glass gradually fell silent... Only Bright remained Bright, independent of time and unharmed. "Immortality" affected both body and mind, and nothing could corrupt Bright—

Correction.

After Bright was infected by 963, nothing could corrupt 963 anymore.

The Foundation’s personnel turnover was so frequent that even old friends who once knew him had long since become blurred faces. He truly could not remember time, nor the names of those around him; both were meaningless to him.

* * *

Bright was quite surprised when he received an email from Alto Clef.

They hadn’t seen each other for a very long time. In Bright’s imagination, Clef was still that inscrutable, decisive, and terrifying executioner. But now, before his eyes, was only an old man with white hair, bent from years of hardship. Just looking at his back, it was hard to recognize him as that Clef.

“I didn’t know the embodiment of Satan could also die of old age,” Bright said as he stood beside him.

Clef laughed as if he had heard something funny, coughing as he laughed, with specks of blood spraying through the air.

Bright didn’t care much. “Hello, Alto,” he greeted calmly, slipping his hand into his pocket and gripping the delicate handle of his gun.

From the moment he saw Clef, Bright knew he had come to kill him.

“Long time no see, Jack,” Clef said. “I’m truly sorry to see you still trapped by 963.”

“So you decided to come personally to resolve this regret, right?” Bright responded shrewdly.

“We’ve discussed this,” Clef glanced at him, his eyes sharp and familiar beneath the brim of his hat. “You are the most unstable factor affecting the Foundation’s future.”

“Because I’m immortal?”

“Because you’re immortal. You will become the most influential member of the Foundation and eventually, over endless time, mutate into something non-human. For that reason, killing you is the last thing I must do. I’m sorry, old friend.”

“Then there’s nothing more to talk about,” Bright said, drawing his right hand from his pocket.

...

“What I want is a peaceful death,” Bright said calmly as he put away his gun, addressing Clef who was still coughing up blood, “not to be locked inside 963 facing endless void. You can’t kill me, Alto.”

He walked away briskly, without looking back.
