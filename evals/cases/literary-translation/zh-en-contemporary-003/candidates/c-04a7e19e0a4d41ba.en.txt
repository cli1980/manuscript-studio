Four

The upper narrative did not let him finish that taunt.

The time loop forced Vodka to lift his weary eyelids, compelled him, shoved his head forward to look at the photograph nailed to the statue's head with five nails.

The photograph was nailed down tight, utterly impossible to peel off, and the material was extremely tough—no matter how he tore or poked at it, it wouldn't damage.

After a series of attempts, Vodka's eyes were tired of glaring. He could only give up, shaking his reddened fingers and panting heavily.

…Fine. This time he really was at his wit's end, with no way out. Human civilization was finished, the end was inexorable.

Wait a minute… Vodka patted his head and thrust both hands into the two front pockets of his pants, rummaging, but couldn't find it. He checked both back pockets too, and came up equally empty.

Finally, he remembered, and in the right pocket of his jacket found what he was looking for—the flint wheel lighter he usually used to light his cigarettes—and set the photograph on fire.

Come on, was that really so hard to think of? Even if he hadn't had a lighter, the Foundation could have employed far more efficient measures: a flamethrower, a rocket launcher, an automated turret, even thaumaturgic protocols—any large-area or small-area destructive weapon, whatever, would be more than enough to obliterate an ordinary photograph beyond all trace.

Could your upper narrative really not think of that?

Five

Another new cycle, entirely expected—it was practically like a child throwing a tantrum. If Vodka didn't lose, the upper narrative simply wouldn't be satisfied.

Vodka truly couldn't be bothered to open his eyes anymore. He was sick to death of this endlessly repeating farce, and even faintly looked forward to just letting 173 snap his neck, then letting the world go ahead and be destroyed—after all, this was just some hastily improvised story.

But even after waiting a long while with his eyes shut, Vodka never received 173's neck massage.

Apparently, the upper narrative was compelling Vodka. It forced Vodka to play this game of wits with it here (if this even qualified as a game of wits), forced Vodka to confront the problem before his eyes.

It wanted Vodka to lose utterly and completely, to satisfy its own desire for victory, and only after it had its fill of amusement would it snuff out this narrative.

Vodka understood the upper narrative's intentions, yet remained powerless to resist, and could only open his eyes.

One glance was all it took to tell him that this new photograph was surely indestructible.

Because it wasn't fixed by any physical means at all—it simply hovered directly in front of 173's face. Square and perfectly flat, its surface smooth as a restroom floor freshly doused with cleaner, like a two-dimensional window unfolded into three-dimensional space, broadcasting a television program put on by God.

The upper narrative had solidified the photograph using other anomalous properties. This had gone beyond the simple combination of 173 and 096—it was true, outright cheating. By all reason, the lower narrative had already lost this contest.

Unfortunately, since time immemorial, the upper narrative has never had to play by reason with the lower narrative.

Vodka let out a weary, long sigh, took off his standard-issue cleaner's jacket, all the while maintaining strict eye contact, then pulled the jacket over 173's head, wrapped the two sleeves around its neck, and tied a simple knot.

Uh… behold this composite product: a rough, mustard-yellow body, a crumpled orange head. Whoever saw this new 173 might think its head was a scrunched-up orange, or perhaps regard 173 as a monk cultivating in seclusion with a hooded head—but absolutely no one would be able to see the photograph inside that head covering. Put on a hood, the same containment treatment as 096 itself.

Vodka figured he could be counted as a "dead man walking" who had actively offered himself up, but don't pay him any tribute—because it really was too stupid. This was just a fool's solution.
