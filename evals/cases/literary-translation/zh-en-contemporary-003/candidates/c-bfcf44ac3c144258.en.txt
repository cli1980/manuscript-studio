Four

The higher-level narrative didn’t let him finish that sarcastic remark.

The time loop forced Vodka to lift his impatient eyelids, pushing him, pressing his head to look at the photo nailed to the statue’s head with five nails.

The photo was nailed down so tightly it couldn’t be removed at all, and its material was very flexible—no matter how he tore or poked, it couldn’t be damaged.

After a series of attempts, Vodka’s eyes were tired from staring, so he could only shake his reddened fingers, panting, and give up.

...Alright, this time he really was out of tricks; there was nothing he could do. Human civilization was finished, the end was inevitable.

Wait a moment... Vodka patted his head, putting both hands into the two front pockets of his pants to search, but he couldn’t find it anywhere. He then checked the two back pockets of his pants, also to no avail.

Finally, he remembered and found what he wanted in the right pocket of his jacket—it was the rolling lighter he usually used to light cigarettes. Then he tried to burn the photo.

No, isn’t this obvious? Even if he didn’t have a lighter, the Foundation could use much more efficient methods: flamethrowers, rocket launchers, automated turrets, or even thaumaturgic programs—any large or small-scale lethal weapon would be enough to completely destroy an ordinary photo, right?

You higher-level narratives can’t think of that?

Five

Another new cycle, as expected, just like a child throwing a tantrum. If Vodka didn’t lose, the higher-level narrative wouldn’t be satisfied.

Vodka was really too lazy to open his eyes again. He was extremely tired of this endless repetition, even vaguely hoping to be snapped by 173 so the world could be destroyed, after all, this was just a temporarily fabricated story.

But even after waiting with his eyes closed for a long time, Vodka didn’t get the neck massage from 173.

It seemed the higher-level narrative was forcing Vodka. It forced Vodka to play this game with it here (if this could even be called a game), forcing Vodka to face the problem in front of him.

It wanted Vodka to lose wholeheartedly, to satisfy its own desire for victory and defeat, and after enjoying itself, to snuff out this narrative.

Vodka knew the higher-level narrative’s intentions but was still powerless to resist, so he opened his eyes.

Just one glance was enough to tell him that this new photo was definitely indestructible.

Because it wasn’t fixed by any physical means at all; it was directly floating in front of 173’s face. Square-shaped, its surface as smooth as a bathroom floor covered in detergent, like a two-dimensional window unfolded in three-dimensional space, broadcasting a TV program sent by God.

The higher-level narrative had solidified the photo with other anomalous properties, which went beyond simply combining 173 and 096—it was outright cheating. Logically speaking, in this game, the lower-level narrative had already lost.

Unfortunately, since ancient times, the higher-level narrative never had to reason with the lower-level narrative.

Vodka sighed wearily, took off the janitor’s standard-issue coat, while maintaining strict eye contact, then put 173’s head into the uniform, wrapping the two sleeves around its neck, and simply tied a tight knot.

Uh... look at this composite product: a rough, earth-yellow body, an orange, wrinkled head. Whoever saw this new 173 might think its head was a squeezed orange, or perhaps a monk with a covered head in meditation, but absolutely no one could see the photo inside that head. Putting on a hood, it was the same containment treatment as the original 096.

Vodka thought he could be considered a positively devoted “one who goes to die,” but don’t salute him, because it was really too stupid—this was a foolproof solution.
