Of course, T.D had thoroughly studied this part. As a member of the SCP Foundation, being unfamiliar with the regulations was basically courting death. He was just normally worried about whether the other side would twist things.

"My first mission was to deal with a black giant dragon. It was intelligent, called itself the Black King, several thousand meters long, and its fire could melt titanium alloy. It could use all kinds of magic and almost blew up the Three Gorges Dam."

T.D felt this sounded strangely familiar. "Why do I feel like this..."

"Just as you think. The Rating Committee has a special term for this — 'pop culture similarity.' This is basically fatal for SCP evaluations. This Black King is actually quite innocent. If it had more cultural background, like connections to some mythology, or a touching story, or some bizarre special skills, then we'd be in trouble. My superior dragged me to the Three Gorges Reservoir; it was right there, its eyes as big as tanks. Then I pulled out my pistol and fired a shot at it. That was it. None of that 'kotodama' (word spirit), high-temperature flames, or invulnerable dragon scales—I didn't see any of that. So you can rest assured, just walk up boldly and throw a punch or slap at it. Or even kick it if you want."

His superior looked at T.D with disbelief.

"I'm joking. Act cautiously; if you need to draw your gun, then draw it. Don't worry, this is just a cleanup job, like cleaning the lab or giving medicine to the public, not a containment assault."

* * *

A few hours later------

"So? Did you open fire?"

"No."

"Oh, that's normal. But don't be too stingy."

T.D found it hard to believe what had just happened. A majestic angel stood right in front of him, twenty pairs of strange wings twisting space, six arms each holding a longsword burning with blue flames, and three faces speaking words like thunder (he couldn't understand them—maybe chanting scriptures, but unfortunately language wasn't T.D's specialty). Unspeakable golden geometric shapes circled around it, as if ready to spread cognitive hazards worldwide.

The angel looked annoyed, raised six swords high, and shouted thunderously. Somehow, T.D felt this must be some kind of causal distortion attack that could erase your ancestors for eighteen generations, or simply a high-energy attack emitting three-thousand-degree heat to vaporize you; and the reaction he should have had was to face it head-on with a punch. He followed his heart.

So the angel silently turned into a pile of white ash. Then even the ash disappeared, dissipating into the air. As if it had never come.
