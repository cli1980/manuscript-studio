At fourteen, I was still at the age where I would take the names of late-night anime shows seriously. Even the poor special effects of blurry blood splattering in movies would make me scream and hide at the other end of the room. But the credibility of classmate Shiina’s words was an even more debatable topic.

I knew which girls in the class would call her a “weirdo” behind her back, but after a brief attempt, I realized the reality that I was powerless to stop. The hostility in childhood actually stemmed from something quite simple: not being ready with pocket money after school on Friday to join a group and take the train to Shibuya 109 for shopping was a symbol of choosing to be isolated. And Shiina Saeko, a member of the go-home club, seemed destined to be completely disconnected from these lifestyle embellishments even before the wave of trends swept into the school. At the same time, even for me, who was already familiar with her, it wasn’t always easy to perfectly handle the occasional wild ideas and seemingly lacking basic common sense in Shiina’s remarks, and in the end, I could only laugh and cry helplessly as I tapped her smooth forehead with a light knock.

But that day, Shiina didn’t go straight home after school as usual. Instead, she pulled me along, dodging the teacher tidying up lesson plans and the patrolling security guard in the corridor, carefully tiptoeing upstairs to hide in the quiet shadow of the rooftop under the setting sun. At first, I was anxious about the punishment hanging over my head and my parents’ scolding, until I saw reflected in my eyes the four-cornered scarf and pleated skirt breaking free from their constraints, falling to the ground. The originally tight, closed loop of the water-blue collar line separated along with it. Only then did I realize that this school uniform might not be Shiina’s size—the faded, oversized fabric covered skin that had developed tiny bumps from contact with the outside world. But my attention was more drawn to a strange object embedded on her chest.

The pure white crystal refracted rainbow light inside, fixed in a nearly adhesive manner by the surrounding skin and faintly blue veins beneath Shiina’s collarbone at her chest. From it spread white gauze fabric, wrapping her unexpectedly slender body in a way I had never seen before—except for the color, it was almost indistinguishable from the heroic magical girls shown on TV.

I did briefly suspect that all this might be a hallucination caused by hay fever, but my youthful spirit helped my body overcome the distortion of the extraordinary, and I reached out first to touch the outline of the crystal. Shiina told me it was a moonstone, proof that she was a magical girl, and that this would soon become a little secret shared only between the two of us.

“How wonderful, I want to become a magical girl too…”

At the end of the fragments of memory, I tirelessly touched that moonstone, leaning against the rooftop railing with Shiina as we waited for the sun to completely set beyond the horizon; the cherished friend and the admired screen image overlapped and merged. The joy brought by entrusting her true identity to me had completely evaporated my reason, leaving me only with a trace of envy and excitement as I repeated the same sigh.

Shiina, however, just gently stroked the tips of my hair, hiding her expression in the shadow cast by the backlight, saying nothing.
