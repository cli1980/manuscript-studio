Connor knew the time had come—not that the timing was right, but that the mission deadline was approaching.

He had spent six months improving the protective suit in the lab before being transferred to the protein synthesis team, leaving him very little time. Yet he still managed to complete the documentation on protein synthesis technology, detailing every step meticulously. Now, all that remained was to transmit the information to the Foundation—the final step. In theory, as long as he handed the documents over to the Foundation, the mission would be considered a success.

In theory.

Reality was far harsher than he had anticipated before joining the GOC. The GOC’s control over researchers with access to classified information was comparable to the Foundation’s containment of Keter-class anomalies. Escape? Every access point was connected to an alarm system, and the GOC’s armed forces were far stronger than the Foundation’s. The network? Every message sent was subject to censorship, making it nearly impossible for him to contact Serena or the Foundation. Confess? That would be suicide. Better to swallow a USB drive and play dead, wait for agents to drag him out and bury him, and then have the Foundation exhume the grave. Interesting, but only if he could convincingly fake death—and they would check the body.

Every time he tore off a page from the calendar, his heart sank. It was now the last day before the mission deadline, exactly one year since he left the Foundation.

Connor’s thoughts were a tangled mess, like a gun pressed against his head. He knew there was no hope of completing the mission on time. Should he just stay with the GOC? The treatment wasn’t bad. No, he knew he wasn’t that kind of person; he could never betray the Foundation, especially with Serena still waiting for him at Site-18. Memories flooded back, many voices intertwined in his mind, joining this internal trial. There was Whitmore’s voice, Carter’s voice, and Serena’s voice.

“We provide global corpse delivery—prepaid!”

“This thing can withstand strong acids and alkalis.”

“If you betray us, I’ll cut open your belly.”

Connor held his head, the vast laboratory empty and silent, the quiet suffocating him. Finally, he broke free from this absurd trial, suddenly lifting his head and scanning the walls—

Several mercury thermometers.

The silver liquid columns reflected a cold light, all showing the same temperature: 23°C.

Outside several buildings, on the other side, Carter’s phone rang.

DNA is the genetic material of the human body; it controls protein synthesis.
If a cell is likened to the Foundation, then DNA is equivalent to the O5 Council, and proteins are like tactical teams. However, DNA is stored in the nucleus, while proteins are synthesized in the cytoplasm.
Therefore, only when DNA transmits information to the cytoplasm can proteins be synthesized, and this requires a medium.

One morning, Serena received an email in her personal inbox—not the one she used for work.

She opened the email; it was an anonymous message:

Dear Ms. Serena Langford:
Connor Langford died while working in the laboratory.
Cause of death: acute mercury poisoning due to an accident.
The coffin will be delivered to your residence today at 14:00.
For compensation matters, please contact staff at █████@goc.com.

Her fingers froze, hovering over the keyboard. The light from the screen reflected in her pupils, her gaze as still as a pool of dead water.

Serena unplugged the computer and sat there in a daze.

That afternoon, Foundation agents transported the coffin back to Site-18. Serena saw Connor’s face—pale and calm, lips slightly blue, with an eerily relaxed expression.

Site Director Hayes stood beside her, placing a hand on her shoulder.

“I’m very sorry, Dr. Langford.”

Her voice was soft, as if speaking to herself: “He must have brought something back…”

Then, tears suddenly broke free, and she trembled as she said:

“He must have brought something back! He must have brought something back!”
