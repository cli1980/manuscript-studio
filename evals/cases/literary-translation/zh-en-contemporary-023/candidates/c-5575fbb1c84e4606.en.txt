You are a drug-addicted prostitute. You walk as close to the edge of the building as possible, trying not to let the cold midnight rain soak your pajamas. The withdrawal symptoms have already begun; you smell the metallic scent of blood in your nostrils, and your convulsing legs make every step agonizing. You know what the antidote is—most of your mind is urging you to go back, back to that dark, unfinished apartment, 15th floor, the second door from the left, where the tattooed bald man will give you a hit. You crave that white powder, which looks like both salt and sugar; one dose, and it’s heaven, bliss. But you can’t—you don’t have a single cent on you, and you were just kicked out. Your heart pounds wildly, stomach acid rises, and you swallow hard, the entire esophagus burning as if it’s sizzling.

A car passes by, its headlights glaring straight into your eyes. You lose your sight in an instant, waving your hands in the air in a daze. When you regain your footing, you find that most of your body is covered in sticky rainwater. You take a shortcut into an alley, dark and foul-smelling, where a swarm of rats scurries past right in front of you. But you don’t care anymore; you don’t want to take another step. You just want to find a dry place to lie down and leave everything to fate. A shadow writhes in a corner; you scream and see a decayed humanoid curled up there. You look at its hands covered in rotten flesh, its chest so deeply hollowed that you can see the bones, its eyes a cloudy milky white, speckled as if moldy. It has no mouth; where its mouth should be is just a black hole. It sees you, and that black hole begins to make sounds:

“Ah... ah... ah...”

That’s all the sounds it can make. The hollow throat noises hover on the boundary between human and inhuman.

“Ah... ah... ah...”

You are terrified. You are scared. You run away with all your might. Its voice keeps echoing behind you.

You cry. You glimpse your own fate; your mind buzzes, and you can see that future you lying in the corner, screaming without a voice. You know—you are trash, the lowest-level junkie in this city. Your body, full of holes, has no resistance; “cancer” can manipulate you at will. You have already seen the endless hell waiting ahead of you; you are sliding down fast, with nothing to hold onto. You keep running, running, running. You rush into your rented room and take out your keys to open the door. The keys fall to the ground; you scream, tears mixing with rain smearing your face. Picking up the keys, you finally open the door. You collapse inside, leaning against the door, crying uncontrollably.

What can you do? What are you able to do? No way out, no antidote. You have handed yourself over to a dead end.

No. You remember. There is still one way.

You shakily walk to the corner of the room. There is a rice cooker, two plates of cold, sour bok choy, and a kitchen knife. You pick up the knife and point it at your wrist. You are very scared; your nose starts bleeding. Are you ready? Are you really ready? You are not ready. You have never been ready for anything. But you have no choice. What other choice do you have? You need to see blood; this is your only way out.

You slash down.

Your scream echoes throughout the rented room, unending. But in this slashing night rain, in this slum where buildings are haphazardly stacked and wires crawl like spider webs, whose attention can a hoarse scream from a woman possibly draw?
