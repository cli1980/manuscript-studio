A girl opens her eyes, awakening in this world that seems like a paradise.  
She stares blankly at the sky, dazzling like shattered glass; on the vast meadow, the wind blows, making the knee-high grass sway, carrying the clouds of the sky as they drift toward the distance.

“This place is…”  
Then, a soap bubble appears in her hand. She pokes it but cannot burst the bubble.  
Behind her, hundreds, even thousands of such soap bubbles gather into a sea of rainbow colors, flying upward with the wind.  
Isn’t it beautiful?

No.  

This place has never been an eternal paradise.  
—And you should have known this all along.

“滴,” “滴,” “滴”...  
A huge discordant sound echoes in the sky. It matches the girl’s heartbeat so perfectly that her heart tightens involuntarily.  
“Wh-what’s happening?”  
Huge, lightning-like straight lines stretch across the entire sky, emitting a buzzing “滴滴” sound.  
The soap bubbles stop flying; some quickly descend to the ground, others burst before they can land.  
The girl gradually feels a force of gravity pressing her down, forcing her to crawl on the ground.  
She tries to look up but can no longer help but lie down on the grass.

“No…”  
Until she reaches out her hand, trying to catch the last bubble.  
And so, the last bubble—the dream of paradise—shatters.

* * *

A girl opens her eyes, awakening in this dim, pure white hell.  
She stares blankly at the ceiling illuminated by the instrument display screens; her hand, still attached to electrodes, tries to grasp something.  
Scientific instruments and mystical arrays are mixed and spread around the bed where the girl lies, as if treating her as a sacrifice to something, or desperately trying to sustain her life.  
The girl wears a patient gown, with an ID badge bearing the Foundation’s emblem. Only when the breathless girl sees this ID does she recall everything about reality—

Sigurrós Stefánsdóttir

This is her name.

“Ah... ahmm... uh...”  
The girl tries to speak but finds her throat can only produce simple sounds.  
She has been unconscious in this hospital bed for a very long time—so long that even after waking, no one from the Foundation comes to her bedside.

“I want to... find everyone...”  
The girl tries to get out of bed but forgets she has been artificially comatose for ten years—her increased height and weakened muscles cannot support her balance, and she falls to the ground, knocking over instruments and bottles.  
Like a paraplegic falling from a wheelchair, she struggles to crawl on the floor, trying to reach the “call” button that should be easy for her to reach. But she cannot, because the nutrients infused into her veins cannot replace the energy needed for movement.

She crawls back to the bed, then slowly curls up on her side, crying and wailing.  
She doesn’t understand why the Foundation abandoned her; the few mentors she knew, who were like parents, seem to be gone.  
And so her story stalls here.  
She has been forgotten by the Foundation—even her final contributions to the Foundation were stopped by her “father” himself.

At this moment, still crying, a new wish is born in her mind:  
“Whoever you are, please take me away from here.”
