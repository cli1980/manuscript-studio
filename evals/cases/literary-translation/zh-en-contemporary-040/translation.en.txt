The frail opens her eyes. She wakes up in the world, a world as if a paradise.

She stares at the horizon at a loss, shining like shattered glass; wind blows in the vast prairie, swaying the knee-high grasses, along with the iridescence of skies, flowing to the distant.

“Where is this……?”

And after, a soap bubble appears in her palm. She pokes it, yet it doesn’t burst out.

Behind her, bubbles like this are countless, emerging into a prismatic sea, to fly high to the horizon.

It was beautiful, wasn’t it?

No.

It was never an eternal paradise.

——Yet you should’ve known better.

“Beep,” “Beep,” “Beep”……

Terrific dissonance echoes in the air. It’s too synchronized with her heartbeat. She is scared.

“Wh……What’s up?”

A strike of straight lines cross through the whole world. Huge, buzzing, as if lightning.

The bubbles stagnate as well. They either fall onto the ground, or shatter apart even before that.

The girl slowly feels a force of gravity, pulling her onto the ground.

She wants to look up, yet she can’t. She’s already on the meadow.

“No……”

Until she reaches her hand, trying to grab the last lather.

And so the last latherdream of paradise is broken.

* * *

The frail opens her eyes. She wakes up in a hellscape, a dull, white hellscape.

She stares at the ceiling lit by the screen, the hand stuck with electrodes tries to grab something still.

Scientific apparatus are set next to the bed, along with thaumaturgic arrays, as if taking her as a sacrific oblation to something, as if using all to hold her life.

A Foundation logo is on her hospital gown. She sees ID badge, and finally recalls all about reality——

Sigurrós Stefánsdóttir

“Ah……eh……em……”

The girl wants to say something, yet she immediately discovers that her throat could only form some simple syllables.

She had long since held unconscious on this sickbed, too long that no one from the Foundation gets to her even when she wakes up.

“I have to……find them……”

The girl tries to get down to the ground, but she forgets that she had been intentionally kept in a coma for ten years——her height had grown, yet her muscles were atrophying. She fails to maintain her balance, then falls straight to the ground, along with the apparatus and bottles.

Crawling as if a paraplegic patient falling off from the wheelchair, she wants to press the “call” button. It was should have been well within her grasp, yet she can’t. The nutrients in her vessel do not replace energy required for movements.

She crawls back to the bed, slowly curls her body up. And cries. And weeps.

She doesn’t know why the Foundation discards her. Those mentors of hers, as close as parents, seem to have left for long.

So then her story stagnates here.

She was forgotten by the Foundation——Even the last wisp of devotion was stopped by her “father.”

At a point, a wish emerges in tears:

“Bring me out from here, no matter who.”
