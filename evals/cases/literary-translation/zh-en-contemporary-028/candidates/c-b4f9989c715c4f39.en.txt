But that is all in the past.

At this moment, the doctor silently picked up a piece of salted meat, bypassed the pointed beak of the mask, and delivered the food into the shadow where the mask met the neck. The fingertips, wrapped in thick skin, pushed the piece of meat into the shadow; the meat seemed to dissolve inch by inch as it disappeared. As the food gradually went deeper, the shadow twisted and contracted slightly, looking like the esophagus squeezing and pushing downward, or like a quick chewing motion. The piece of salted meat soon vanished below the collar.

The doctor swallowed saliva.

Then, the taste sensation belatedly surged back.

Of course, it had not expected the salted meat to be as tender as stewed game meat, but this meat seemed a bit too salty. The moment the meat touched the esophagus, it absorbed the mucous membrane’s fluids, causing an unusual dryness.

Along with the rich salty flavor filling the taste buds, there was also a hint of distinctive smoky aroma. Although the pork had been cured, the oily fat fragrance masked the bitterness caused by excessive salt. Overall, it was not too bad.

The returning taste sensation lasted briefly and soon faded away.

But the salted meat alone was astringent and monotonous.

It then picked up a thin biscuit, broke the long biscuit into several pieces, stacked them together, and stuffed them into the shadowy esophagus.

This might not have been the best choice either. Although it had broken the biscuit into small pieces as much as possible, the hard and dry edges of the biscuit still scratched the inner wall of its throat with their sharp parts. Although the muscular contractions of the esophagus replaced chewing, grinding the biscuit into finer fragments, the uncomfortable scraping sensation still dullly persisted below the chest.

The doctor paused the action of picking up food, then reached for the water jug beside it.

The water moistened the dry mucous membranes and helped push the hard biscuit pieces into the stomach, greatly relieving the discomfort. But it couldn’t always eat by swallowing with water like this.

With the food swallowed, a light wheat fragrance finally rose slowly, filling the doctor’s senses. This biscuit was far better than previous ones; lacking the bitterness caused by historical and technological limitations was not a drawback. It had never liked that bitterness anyway. Fortunately, the Foundation’s food rations did not strictly pursue historical authenticity.

In the end, it chose to sandwich the salted meat and biscuit between pieces of broken cheese and swallow the whole thing.

In this way, although the double-layered sandwich was somewhat large for the narrow throat, the lubrication from the cheese made it easier to swallow and less strenuous.

The doctor began to feel grateful that the Foundation had chosen a type of cheese that was not the fully hard kind with a waxy texture. It carefully divided the food bit by bit and pushed the mini sandwich into the shadow.

Perhaps it was still a bit too large? The beak part of the doctor’s mask subtly curved upward, feeling the food being squeezed and swallowed by the esophagus, then it shrugged lightly.
