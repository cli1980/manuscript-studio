3,000,000 Years

Walking into the corner café, I met Talloran as promised at the seat by the floor-to-ceiling window. At that moment, he looked up at me; the sunlight shone brightly upon him, and his green eyes reminded me of the waves of a storm petrel, emerald crests crashing onto the shore, about to splash white stars.

Have you ever been out to sea?

His gaze passed through me, looking at a clear autumn sky where water and sky merged into one. On the swaying, unstable deck, there were only two people; Draven leaned against the railing beside him and suddenly laughed. He looked far away, where a fountain spouted high above the water, its faint, lingering sound drifting in the air. But Talloran only saw vast, magnificent illusions: the city of Atlantis standing in the air, coral spires refracting a silky red light, bricks deep purple yet shrouded in the colors of dusk, perfectly harmonious.

He blinked, and Draven had already come close to his eyes, gently kissing his cheek. He stared at the agent’s face until it began to bleed, becoming like his dead sister abandoned at the end of an alley, surrounded by mosquitoes, flies, and sewage. His sister spoke, singing the mournful day of the requiem, then she appeared younger and smaller, curled in the corner of a bedroom, clutching a torn new dress. Moths struggled out from between her lips and teeth, fluttering toward a candle; the moment their scaled wings touched the flame, the glow shone brightly; ashes and candle wax mixed together, dark gray and ivory white loving each other until death.

The candle continued to burn. From that fall of light, lush fragrances grew and spread. He tasted bitterness in his mouth, like holding a coin; then the sweet metallic taste of blood, parasites gnawing at his liver, piercing his skin, poking out to deeply inhale the salty, astringent sea breeze—“whoosh”—passing over him, like the cold fins of a distant school of fish brushing his hairline, leaves drawing a golden river on the mountaintop. Blades grew in the wind, gouging out his eyes, so those two phosphorescent stones rolled down and shattered on the ground into uniquely strange art.

Parasites crawled toward his wrists, praising the twisted veins there, no different from the lapis lazuli sleeping in the pearl veins. They devoured the blood vessels; dark blood dripped from his fingertips, drawing concentric circles and three inward-pointing arrows on the rocking ship. They laughed like the venomous serpent of Eden flicking its tongue, hiss—hiss—

Talloran closed his eyes. The dying sun, its image projected on the inside of his eyelids, evoked a shiver. His fetal dreams reappeared in the dim light of the star: one cell devouring another, peptide chains continuously entangling and folding, painting fractal patterns, and finally giving birth to new parallel universes and lower narratives. He hid in the cracks of time and space, glimpsing pale blonde hair. Small red lights lit up, one, and countless more.

Name: James Martin Talloran. Age: unimportant... Draven, where is Draven? I need him, I need you.

You should try to listen, MottHery. Talloran spoke to me while finishing his coffee; the mug was as blue as the unpolluted ocean. I had understood the absurdity of life’s revelry, I had known the pleasures six feet under. I will become your narrator, writing your lifetime of pain, letting you write the end of Talloran.

I obeyed willingly, silent and listening. His world unfolded before me.
