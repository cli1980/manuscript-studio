The feeling of having a body again was very strange. Bright locked eyes with the emerald green eyes of the person in the mirror, still somewhat unable to come to his senses.

"It was made according to the earliest memory in your deep consciousness," the machine explained. "We could only dig this far; it might not be exactly alike."

"That's enough," he shrugged.

What do you plan to do next?

"To be a good person to the end, how about sending me an interstellar spaceship?"

* * *

As the machine grandly set up an entire production line to reproduce the space industry, Bright became a thoroughly idle man, wandering aimlessly every day in this metallic world. The planet's atmosphere was thin, and its composition was quite different from Earth's. When particle streams blowing from the depths of the starry sky struck the planet's surface, they created a splendid spectacle.

The wind surged with deep, gentle electromagnetic waves, as if something grand was streaking across the night sky, rustling softly.

One day, as usual, he lay on the ground watching the stars when a voice gently nudged him in his mind. Compared to before, this consciousness was softer, carrying a warmth different from the machine's, which surprised Bright a little.

"We still have gender distinctions," the voice chirped, explaining. "You are now conversing with 4.2% of the total population, the vast majority of whom are female."

"Here's some news for you. We found that your pendant still preserves all the erased consciousnesses, though only fragments remain, they have always been there."

"You carry the museum of human civilization, child."

"Hehe, don't be stubborn. In terms of time, you are the ancestor of all existing humans; but as a prehistoric life suddenly arriving under the vast stars of the galactic era, haven't you ever felt the child's confusion and curiosity?"

Another wave of consciousness noisily surged up. "Our branch has settled in this star system for too long and can no longer turn back, but you can still find the way out."

"Go between the star seas, seek out the rest of humanity, explore the vast universe, and see the boundless beautiful scenery."

"Child, oh child," their delicate fragmented voices echoed in his mind, "may starlight illuminate your path forward."

...

The day of departure arrived.

"One last question," the machine asked. "What is your name?"

"Does it really matter?" Bright retorted.

"It determines how you will be recorded in our records."

Bright thought for a moment.

"When I was still human... I was called Jack Bright, doctor or personnel supervisor. In the Foundation's records, I am SCP-963, 'Immortal'—but I am tired of immortality."

He spoke slowly and deliberately. "There was an old abolished designation; they called her 'Child of Man.' I think you can also call me..."

The man who was once human fell silent for a moment.

The machine waited patiently.

"'Soul of Man.'"

The earth trembled beneath his feet and receded, as if in a breath, the dark night sky rushed toward him, just as he had fallen into the embrace of the pendant countless times before. But this time, he no longer felt cold.

The soul of man fell deep into the stars.
