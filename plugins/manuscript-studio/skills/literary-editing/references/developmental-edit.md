# Developmental Edit

Use this workflow for story architecture, argument or narrative structure, character arcs, pacing, point of view strategy, and other manuscript-scale concerns.

## Scope and readiness

A final developmental verdict requires the complete manuscript in reading order. Chapter-level work may gather evidence or test a local revision, but it remains `provisional` until the whole manuscript and its dependencies have been reviewed.

Confirm the workspace review identity, source import identity, manuscript manifest, project brief, and any prior assessment. If approved structural changes are already underway, identify the revision baseline and do not mix incompatible versions.

## Review method

1. Read the complete manuscript before fixing on a diagnosis.
2. Build a working map of chapters, scenes or sections, viewpoint, chronology, major turns, character arcs, and unresolved threads.
3. Evaluate:
   - premise, stakes, causality, and escalation;
   - plot and subplot architecture;
   - character goals, motivation, agency, and change;
   - scene purpose, conflict, turn, and aftermath;
   - pacing, suspense, repetition, and compression;
   - point of view, narrative distance, and information release;
   - theme, motif, emotional logic, and genre promise;
   - world rules, chronology, and continuity;
   - opening, midpoint development, climax, and ending in relation to the work's aims.
4. Use named story frameworks only as optional diagnostic lenses. Never force the manuscript into a beat sheet or formula the author did not choose.
5. Test each recommendation against downstream effects elsewhere in the manuscript.

## Verdict and recommendation records

Return the verdict before any intervention. Record each proposed structural action as `DEV-001`, `DEV-002`, and so on, including:

- priority and affected locations;
- observed issue and evidence;
- recommended structural operation or author decision;
- intended narrative effect;
- dependencies and likely cascade effects;
- alternatives when they represent materially different strategies;
- acceptance status.

Developmental recommendations are not sentence-level diffs by default. Deterministic operations such as moving or cutting a clearly identified block may be represented as bounded proposals. New scenes, broad rewrites, or reconceived arcs require an approved strategy and a separately scoped drafting proposal; approval of the diagnosis is not open-ended permission to rewrite.

## Approval and application

The author may approve, reject, or defer recommendations independently. Before applying an approved deterministic operation, revalidate both review identities, the workspace location, and dependencies. Apply only the accepted operation, update the continuity records, and deliver it according to the operating mode.

After substantial author revision, mark affected downstream line-edit, copyedit, and proofing results stale. Resume from the new Git commit rather than carrying old locations forward by assumption.

## Completion

A developmental edit is complete only after the entire manuscript, its major arcs, and cross-manuscript consequences have been reviewed. Chapter reports alone cannot support a whole-book completion claim.
