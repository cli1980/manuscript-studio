# Proofreading

Use this workflow for final error detection in a formatted proof after substantive editing and copyediting are complete.

## Readiness

Proofreading requires a stable, paginated or otherwise formatted proof such as PDF, print-ready pages, or a production-layout document. If only editable manuscript text is available, do not label the work proofreading; route it to copyediting or explicitly label it a limited surface-error review.

Record the proof version, workspace review identity, source or production identity, page count, and approved manuscript version used for comparison. Confirm whether this is the first proof or a correction round.

## Review method

For a first proof, read the complete publication word by word and inspect layout. Check:

- typographical errors, missing or duplicated text, and corrupted characters;
- implementation of approved edits and accidental regressions;
- headers, footers, folios, running heads, and chapter openers;
- indents, spacing, scene breaks, ornaments, and paragraph treatment;
- bad line or page breaks, widows, orphans, stacks, and collisions;
- contents, cross-references, notes, captions, links, and numbering;
- font, emphasis, punctuation, and other style consistency;
- discrepancies between the approved manuscript and proof.

For later rounds, check every marked correction and its fallout, then inspect affected pages and production elements. Do not casually reopen settled style or developmental choices. Record serious upstream problems as production queries with their impact.

## Verdict and correction proposals

Return a proof verdict before corrections. Record each proposed correction as `PR-001`, `PR-002`, and so on, with page or production location, exact current content, proposed correction, category, severity, rationale, and status. For layout problems, describe the visible defect and desired outcome without prescribing unsupported production mechanics.

Do not modify the proof or source manuscript during review.

## Approval and application

The author or production owner may approve individual IDs or a named proof batch. Revalidate the proof version and location before applying. Apply only approved corrections, record what changed, and create a new workspace revision. Update the source only through the selected operating mode; never overwrite the reviewed proof.

## Completion

Proofing may be batched operationally, but `whole-book-complete` requires every page and production element in scope. After corrections, perform a final verification pass for implementation and correction fallout before release.
