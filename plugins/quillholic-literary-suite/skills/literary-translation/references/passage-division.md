# Passage Division

Use this procedure during the whole-chapter reading, before passage translation begins.

## Governing Principle

Divide a chapter into the largest coherent units that can be translated and reviewed closely without breaking a continuous narrative, emotional, rhetorical, or conversational movement.

Coherence determines boundaries. Length only controls manageability.

## 1. Map Natural Turns

Mark meaningful changes in one or more of these dimensions:

- scene, location, or time;
- viewpoint or narrative distance;
- speaker or phase of conversation;
- character objective or power relation;
- action, reflection, description, or exposition;
- mood or emotional pressure;
- subject, argument, or train of thought;
- revelation, decision, reversal, or structural function.

A paragraph break alone is not a sufficient reason to divide a passage.

## 2. Preserve Continuous Movements

Do not divide inside:

- a sustained action sequence;
- a closely connected dialogue exchange;
- a continuous train of thought;
- a description built around one developing image;
- the preparation for a revelation, confrontation, reversal, or symbolic recurrence, the decisive moment, and its immediate response;
- a rhetorical sequence whose force depends on accumulation;
- a transition whose meaning depends on both sides.

If splitting a unit would require reconstructing its momentum during review, keep it together.

## 3. Form the Largest Manageable Units

Group paragraphs around one dominant movement. Then test manageability:

- If a unit contains too many distinct translation problems for focused review, split it at its strongest internal turn.
- If a unit is too slight to establish tone, rhythm, or context, combine it with the adjacent movement it serves.
- Never force passages into a uniform word or character count.

A passage must be large enough to preserve local context and small enough for two-path drafting and issue-by-issue dialogue.

## 4. Attach Transitions by Function

- Keep an anticipatory transition with what follows.
- Keep a concluding or reflective transition with what precedes.
- If a sentence deliberately bridges both movements, keep it in the passage it primarily serves. Provide context for the next passage through metadata or by loading the preceding ending; do not duplicate the sentence in the next passage's source-text body.

## 5. Validate Each Boundary

For every proposed break, ask:

1. Does the preceding passage have one dominant movement?
2. Can its local purpose and tone be understood without translating the next passage?
3. Does its ending create a natural pause rather than an interruption?
4. Does the next passage begin with a recognizable shift?
5. Would separation damage rhythm, suspense, dialogue, argument, or emotional continuity?
6. Is each resulting unit manageable for exact translation and focused review?

If separation causes the damage described in question 5, move or remove the boundary.

## Output

Produce a concise complete passage map identifying each unit by filename, opening and closing words, and a short structural reason for its boundary. Present it as a proposal, explicitly request the user's approval of the complete map, and stop. Do not translate or extract the passages while approval is pending.

If the user questions, qualifies, or proposes any boundary change, treat the response as negotiation rather than partial approval. Redo the passage-division step using that feedback, present the entire revised map, and request explicit approval again. Repeat until the user approves the current complete map. Only then follow Step 2 in `SKILL.md` to create and validate the internal passage set.

Treat boundaries as fixed once Step 3 begins for either affected passage. If translation exposes a structural problem that genuinely requires a later change, explain the issue and re-enter Step 2 with approval pending. Present the complete revised map and obtain explicit approval before changing source-passage files or invalidating any downstream state. After approval, update the map and source-passage files, invalidate or rebuild affected checkpoints and review state, rerun the Step 2 completeness checks, and repeat Step 3 for every affected passage. Never silently discard finalized wording or decisions.
